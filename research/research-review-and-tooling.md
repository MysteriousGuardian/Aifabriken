## Research conclusions to retain, weaken or reject

1. **Retain:** technical constraints are documented, while unmet customer needs remain unvalidated. Describe the result as “our inspected source set did not establish first-hand evidence of the selected handoff/recovery difficulty.” Do not say no such evidence exists publicly.
2. **Weaken “already solved”:** show an “Existing alternatives” panel, not proof that consumable selection is solved. Product listings establish availability, not adequacy. Likewise, failure to find a diagnostic aid does not establish novelty or that all existing aids act only before cutting.
3. **Keep ravel diagnosis as a hypothesis:** a panel tracing possible cut-edge problems to readiness, consumable condition and setup could be a useful engineer demo. Cause links must retain their own evidence status. Do not display an unsupported definite diagnosis or prescribe safe corrective actions.
4. **Do not let other-model evidence raise selected-job confidence:** the reported X2500 repair lead should stay separate from the Soff-Cut 150 job. A residential thread or generic demolition article should not silently become direct support for early-entry recovery.
5. **Correct the proposed experiment:** photos alone may not reveal cause. Disagreement may reflect missing context; agreement may be confidently wrong. Collect supervised cases with known context and, where feasible, independently established causes. Compare expert judgments with that reference, record uncertainty and whether a proposed aid improves decisions. Low agreement is a reason to investigate, not proof of demand; high agreement is not by itself a reason to discard a product idea.
6. **Keep at most five active opportunities:** the supplied expanded JSON has six. Treat the original O02 framing as superseded if a new O06 diagnosis hypothesis is explored; retain O04 as backlog. Keep historical IDs. Describe editorial priority separately from arithmetic score order, or recompute ordering with a documented tie-break.
